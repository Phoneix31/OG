What I have learnt as a Photographer over the years

For Photographers

The Importance of Lighting

One of the first lessons I learned in photography is that lighting can make or break an image. Whether it’s natural light or artificial, understanding how light interacts with a subject is crucial. The golden hours—just after sunrise and before sunset—offer the most flattering light for outdoor photography. Additionally, mastering how to use shadows and highlights can add depth and emotion to an image.

Composition Matters

A well-composed image draws the viewer’s attention effortlessly. The rule of thirds, leading lines, and framing techniques help in creating visually appealing photographs. Over time, I have learned to break these rules creatively to achieve unique compositions that tell a compelling story.

The Power of Patience

Photography, especially in genres like wildlife and street photography, requires immense patience. Some of the best shots come from waiting for the right moment. Whether it’s capturing the perfect expression on a person’s face or waiting for the right lighting conditions, patience always pays off.

Editing Enhances

Post-processing is an essential part of modern photography, but I have learned that editing should enhance an image rather than fix fundamental mistakes. A well-exposed and well-composed photograph requires minimal adjustments, making editing a tool rather than a crutch.

Storytelling is Key

Every photograph should tell a story or evoke an emotion. Whether it’s a portrait, landscape, or candid moment, the most impactful images connect with the viewer on a deeper level. Learning to capture emotions and narratives in my photographs has been one of the most rewarding aspects of my journey.

Never Stop Learning

Photography is constantly evolving, and there is always something new to learn. From experimenting with different styles and techniques to staying updated with new technology, continuous learning has helped me grow as a photographer.
